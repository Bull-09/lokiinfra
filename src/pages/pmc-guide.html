How to hire a PMC for an industrial project in Gujarat

What a project management consultancy does, when to bring one in, how fees work, which approvals to plan for, and how to choose the right firm.

HOME / GUIDES / HIRING A PMC

By Lokesh Shinkar, Founder & Director, LOKI Infra · Reviewed by Sanjay Shinkar, Lifetime Director · Updated 11 October 2026

In short: hire a project management consultancy (PMC) when a factory or plant is too costly to leave to contractors managing themselves. Appoint it early, ideally before the design is final. Agree the fee model in writing, and choose a firm with real site experience, independent reporting and people on site every day.

Why industrial projects need independent management

Delays and cost overruns are common even on large, well-funded projects. In India’s own monitoring of central infrastructure projects, the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) reported in March 2024 that 779 of 1,873 monitored projects were running late, by an average of about 36 months, and 449 had exceeded their original cost (MoSPI data, reported by Outlook Business).

Private factory builds face the same causes on a smaller scale: incomplete design at tender stage, weak contract control, late approvals and poorly coordinated trades. A PMC exists to manage those risks for the owner.

What does a PMC do?

A PMC manages the whole project on the owner’s behalf. It does not build. It plans the programme and budget, prepares tenders, coordinates every contractor on site, measures and certifies bills, controls variations, enforces quality and safety, and runs testing, commissioning and handover. How are PMC fees structured?

PMC fees in India are usually set in one of three ways:

  1. Percentage of construction cost. Common for full-lifecycle PMC. The fee scales with the project.
  2. Fixed monthly fee. Common for construction-stage PMC, based on the team deployed and the duration.
  3. Lump sum for a defined scope. Used for a single service, such as tendering or commissioning support.

Whichever model you choose, the fee and the team (names, roles and time on site) should be agreed in writing before work starts.

Which approvals should a factory project in Gujarat plan for?

A PMC does not replace your licensing consultant, but it should build these approvals into the programme so that none of them holds up construction or start-up. Common ones for a new factory in Gujarat include:

  • Consent to Establish (CTE) from the Gujarat Pollution Control Board (GPCB), before construction starts
  • Consolidated Consent and Authorisation (CC&A) from GPCB, before production starts
  • Factory plan approval and factory licence under the Factories Act, through the Directorate of Industrial Safety and Health (DISH), Gujarat
  • Building plan approval and building use permission from the local authority, or from GIDC for plots in GIDC estates
  • Fire NOC from the fire department
  • Power connection from the local distribution company (DGVCL in South Gujarat, including Surat)

Requirements depend on industry, location and size. Confirm the exact list with the authorities or a licensing consultant for your project.

How to choose a PMC: an 8-point checklist

  1. Relevant site experience. Ask for industrial or commercial projects of similar size and type.
  2. Independence. The PMC should report only to you, not take commissions from contractors or suppliers.
  3. Named team. Know who will be on site, their experience, and how many days a week they will be there.
  4. Reporting. Ask to see a sample weekly progress report and a cost report.
  5. Cost control method. How do they measure work, certify bills and handle variations?
  6. Quality and safety system. Inspection and test plans, material testing, HSE audits.
  7. Handover standard. As-built drawings, O&M manuals, warranties and a closed snag list.
  8. Clear fee and scope. A written scope of services, a fee model and exclusions.

Questions to ask before you sign

Who exactly will be on my site, and for how long?

Get names, roles and the planned deployment in the contract, not just in the proposal.

How will you report progress and cost?

Expect a weekly progress report against the programme, a monthly cost report with cost-to-complete, and a record of every approved variation.

What happens if the project is delayed?

A good PMC explains how it identifies delays early, how it prepares recovery plans, and how its own fee is affected if the duration changes.

Can you take over a project that has already started?

Many PMCs, including LOKI Infra, can. Expect an initial audit of progress, contracts and bills before a recovery plan.
